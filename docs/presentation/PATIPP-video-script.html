Have you ever built something that worked, but wondered whether you could build it again without help? That feeling is why I made PATIPP.

In a recent interview, I created a Spring Boot project and connected its layers. But I used AI for some of the service and controller logic. It showed me a gap: I could follow the code, but I needed more practice writing, explaining and debugging it myself.

I wanted a place I could open and immediately know what to practise next. Something that would guide me from Java fundamentals to building Spring applications, without spending the whole session deciding what to study.

That became PATIPP: a preparation platform where I can work on what I find difficult, keep a record of my attempts, and come back to practise again.

Here is the experience I am building for myself. I open the Java and Spring Boot challenge and see the next exercise, a relevant resource, and the behaviour my solution should satisfy.

For example: read some sample JSON and build endpoints to add and delete books. I attempt it in my IDE first. If I get stuck, I can reveal hints progressively. Then I run the checks locally and record whether I completed it independently, with hints, or with a solution.

The part I want to make distinctive is returning to something I needed help with and rebuilding it myself. PATIPP suggests an assisted task to revisit, keeps my last reflection nearby, and records when a later attempt is completed independently. For me, that is a more useful sign of progress than simply ticking a lesson off.

I have also put together a 30-day Java and Spring Boot challenge. I have not started it yet. Electricity issues have interrupted my plans, so I am making room for real life: a ten-to-thirty-minute core exercise when I am busy, with a longer extension when I have more time.

I am not claiming I will master Java in thirty days. I want to become more consistent and more capable of building without depending on generated answers. PATIPP is the guide I am building to help me do that. If you have struggled with the same gap, what would help you come back and practise?
